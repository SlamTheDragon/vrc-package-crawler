## 2. What the Crawler Indexes

2.1. **Factual Metadata Only.**  
The crawler collects and indexes only public, objective, factual specifications from publicly accessible storefront pages. Indexed facts include: package names, reverse-DNS identifiers (`com.author.tool`), semantic version numbers, platform compatibility flags (`Unity 2022`, `PhysBones`, `Quest`), pricing, release tags, and canonical storefront URLs.

2.2. **Statutory Copyright Basis for Factual Data.**  
Under 17 U.S.C. Section 102(b), Section 175 of the Intellectual Property Code of the Philippines (Republic Act No. 8293), and the landmark ruling in *Feist Publications, Inc. v. Rural Telephone Service Co.* (499 U.S. 340), raw factual specifications do not carry copyright protection. Such facts include version numbers, technical identifiers, compatibility flags, and pricing. Creative marketing descriptions and lore are truncated to brief functional snippets of at most 256 characters. This prevents copyright appropriation (*Authors Guild v. Google, Inc.*, 804 F.3d 202).

2.3. **Three-State Timestamp Confidence Rubric.**  
The Project records asset creation timestamps under a strict three-state confidence rubric to protect temporal integrity:
- `confirmed`: Directly extracted from authoritative upstream platform metadata (such as GitHub API `created_at` or structured BOOTH microdata).
- `inferred`: Derived from the earliest verified commit or changelog entry.
- `unknown`: When no upstream publication date exists, the timestamp is recorded as `NULL`. The pipeline does not substitute local crawl timestamps for missing upstream publication dates.

2.4. **Public Platforms Indexed.**  
The Project indexes publicly accessible listings from: BOOTH.pm, Gumroad, Jinxxy, itch.io, GitHub, and community package registries such as VRCArena (subject to Section 5).

## 3. What the Crawler Never Indexes

3.1. **Strict Zero-Binary Invariant.**  
The Software does not download, cache, store, unpack, mirror, or redistribute proprietary compiled assets, 3D meshes, textures, or binary code. Prohibited file formats include `.unitypackage`, `.vpmz`, `.zip`, `.tar.gz`, `.rar`, `.7z`, `.fbx`, `.obj`, `.blend`, `.prefab`, `.asset`, `.anim`, raw audio, compiled shaders, and executables (`.dll`, `.exe`, `.so`). Transport-layer streaming socket guardrails inspect incoming `Content-Type` headers. The guardrails abort any HTTP response matching binary archive MIME types. They also abort any response exceeding 5 MB before socket ingestion.

3.2. **Exclusion of Non-Public and Personal Data.**  
The crawler does not access authenticated account dashboards, checkout funnels, private messages, customer order histories, buyer identities, or payment and billing records.

3.3. **No Platform Management API Extraction.**  
Platform storefront management APIs (such as those of Gumroad, Jinxxy, BOOTH, and itch.io) serve seller inventory management, not third-party catalog extraction. The crawler does not use platform management APIs to extract catalog data. It operates as a conventional web crawler against publicly accessible web pages, obeying robots.txt directives as described in Section 6.

3.4. **Zero Credential Use and Unauthenticated Access.**  
The crawler operates strictly as an unauthenticated, logged-out public guest. It contains no credential scrapers, no session token handlers, and no cookie-jar persistence. It bypasses no password gates or technical access barriers.

## 6. Crawling and Technical Safeguards

6.1. **Robots Exclusion Protocol Compliance (IETF RFC 9309).**  
The crawler voluntarily honors the IETF RFC 9309 Robots Exclusion Protocol as an access preference. RFC 9309 states that robots rules are technical preferences stated by website operators, not a form of legal access authorization. The crawler checks `/robots.txt` before fetching any URL path.

It follows all `Disallow` rules, applies the longest prefix matching standard, and caches directives for 24 hours. Voluntary compliance with robots.txt is a good-faith commitment. It is not an admission that disregarding robots directives would be unlawful.

6.2. **Transparent and Actionable User-Agent Identification.**  
All HTTP requests must include an honest `User-Agent` header. This header identifies the project, repository, and administrator contact:
```http
User-Agent: VRCDiscoveryBot/1.0 (+https://github.com/SlamTheDragon/vrc-package-crawler, slamthedragon@gmail.com)
```

6.3. **Host-Isolated AIMD Rate Limiting and Pacing.**  
The crawler applies Additive Increase / Multiplicative Decrease (AIMD) rate limiting per target hostname. Closed storefronts require a minimum delay of 3.0 to 5.0 seconds between requests, plus randomized decorrelated jitter.

6.4. **Zero Perimeter Bypass and Bot Barrier Halting.**  
The Software does not bypass any access perimeter. Security challenges, rate-limit responses, and bot barriers are treated as explicit technical refusals of service. The Software contains no CAPTCHA solvers, no proxy rotation mechanisms, and no browser fingerprint emulation farms.

6.5. **Network Load and Common-Law Claims.**  
The Project uses request pacing, rate limiting, and connection pooling. These measures aim to avoid unreasonable interference with target infrastructure. These measures reduce the risk of claims based on excessive automated access (such as common-law Trespass to Chattels). But they do not guarantee that no claim will occur.

6.6. **Logged-Out Session Air-Gap.**  
Operators must not link any personal platform account to a running crawler instance. Crawler execution must remain fully air-gapped from personal platform sessions.

## 7. Intellectual Property, Metadata, and Visual Search Indexing

7.1. **Factual Data Non-Copyrightability and Original Compilation.**  
Individual factual data do not constitute protected expression under 17 U.S.C. Section 102(b) and Section 175 of Republic Act No. 8293. This includes version numbers, prices, reverse-DNS identifiers, and platform compatibility tags.

Original database schema design, coordination, selection, and taxonomy arrangement can receive protection as compilations. This protection arises under Section 173.2 of Republic Act No. 8293. Nothing in these Terms implies that an entire third-party database or website is uncopyrightable.

7.2. **Visual Search Indexing, Transformative Fair Use, and the Server Test.**  
The visual search pipeline is engineered to locate works without substituting for artistic display. The legal status of visual indexing involves two distinct doctrines:
- **(a) Transformative Fair Use for Search Pointers:** In *Kelly v. Arriba Soft Corp.* (336 F.3d 811), the Ninth Circuit ruled that displaying low-resolution thumbnails to help users find images is transformative fair use. In *Authors Guild v.

Google, Inc.* (804 F.3d 202), the Second Circuit affirmed that functional search snippets do not substitute for original expressive works.
- **(b) The Server Test and the Regional Circuit Split:** In *Perfect 10, Inc. v. Amazon.com, Inc.* (508 F.3d 1146), the Ninth Circuit held that linking to or framing an image hosted on a third-party server does not violate the public display right. However, courts in the Second Circuit (*Goldman v. Breitbart News Network, LLC*, 271 F. Supp.

3d 495, and *Nicklen v. Sinclair Broadcast Group, Inc.*, 551 F. Supp. 3d 188) have rejected the Server Test, holding that embedding can infringe the display right regardless of hosting.

The Ninth Circuit affirmed the Server Test in *Hunley v. Instagram, LLC* (73 F.4th 1060), but acknowledged criticism.
- **(c) Pure Pointer Architecture and Interfacing Applications:** The Project operates strictly as an indexing pointer service. The Project delivers direct origin media URLs (images, videos, YouTube embeds, GIF links) as metadata attachments to the canonical package. The Project does not host, store, or re-serve image or media files on disk or in database records. Downstream interfacing applications (such as desktop package managers) handle caching, downloading, and display on their own end, under their own independent operational and legal responsibilities.

7.3. **Database Compilation and Schema Protection.**  
The Maintainer claims no property in third-party factual metadata. But the relational schema, categorization taxonomy, and curated dataset structures of the Project constitute original compilation work. This compilation is licensed under the terms of this repository.

9.8. **Account-Based Storefront Functions Out of Scope.**

## Appendix A: Table of Authorities, Statutory Compendium, and Legal Jurisprudence

This appendix supplies formal legal citations and jurisprudence supporting the operational boundaries set out in Sections 1 through 18. It gives legal scholarship, transparent architectural grounding, and compliance reference.

### 1. Factual Metadata and Compilation Copyright
- *Feist Publications, Inc. v. Rural Telephone Service Co.*, 499 U.S. 340, 111 S. Ct. 1282, 113 L. Ed. 2d 358 (1991). The Court held that raw facts and alphabetical telephone directories lack copyright protection because they lack original creative authorship. Only original selection, coordination, and arrangement receive protection, with a "thin" copyright scope.
- 17 U.S.C. Section 102(b). The statute states that copyright protection for an original work of authorship does not extend to any idea, procedure, process, system, method of operation, concept, principle, or discovery.
- Intellectual Property Code of the Philippines, Rep. Act No. 8293, Section 175 (1997). The statute states that no copyright shall subsist in any idea, procedure, system, method or operation, concept, or mere data of itself.
- Rep. Act No. 8293, Section 173.2 (1997). The statute extends copyright protection to collections of data or other materials that by reason of the selection and arrangement of their contents constitute intellectual creations.
- *Authors Guild v. Google, Inc.*, 804 F.3d 202 (2d Cir. 2015). The Second Circuit held that digital scanning and display of short search snippets constitutes transformative fair use because it supplies search functionality without offering a market substitute for the original works.

### 2. Visual Search Indexing, Transformative Fair Use, and Server Doctrines
- *Kelly v. Arriba Soft Corp.*, 336 F.3d 811 (9th Cir. 2003). The Ninth Circuit held that the automated retrieval and display of low-resolution thumbnail images in search results constitutes transformative fair use because thumbnails serve as visual locators guiding users to original source pages.
- *Perfect 10, Inc. v. Amazon.com, Inc.*, 508 F.3d 1146 (9th Cir. 2007). The Ninth Circuit established the "Server Test." A search engine displays or distributes a work only if the photographic image is stored on its own computer hardware. Providing inline links or framing third-party images hosted on remote servers does not constitute direct copyright infringement.
- *Goldman v. Breitbart News Network, LLC*, 271 F. Supp. 3d 495 (S.D.N.Y. 2018). The Southern District of New York rejected the Server Test, holding that embedding or framing a tweet containing a copyrighted image can violate the display right, even if the image remains hosted on a third-party server.
- *Nicklen v. Sinclair Broadcast Group, Inc.*, 551 F. Supp. 3d 188 (S.D.N.Y. 2021). The Southern District of New York reaffirmed its rejection of the Server Test, concluding that embedding constitutes a display under the Copyright Act.
- *Hunley v. Instagram, LLC*, 73 F.4th 1060 (9th Cir. 2023). The Ninth Circuit maintained the Server Test for Instagram embeds, but acknowledged widespread academic and judicial criticism.

### 3. Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA) and Public Data
- *Van Buren v. United States*, 593 U.S. 374, 141 S. Ct. 1638, 210 L. Ed. 2d 26 (2021). The Court adopted a "gates-up versus gates-down" test. It held that CFAA Section 1030(a)(2) applies only to individuals who get information from areas within a computer system to which technical access barriers block access, not to those who misuse information they were permitted to access.
- *hiQ Labs, Inc. v. LinkedIn Corp.*, 31 F.4th 1180 (9th Cir. 2022). The Ninth Circuit held that accessing publicly available web data without bypassing technological access barriers does not constitute access without authorization under the CFAA.
- 18 U.S.C. Section 1030 (Computer Fraud and Abuse Act).

### 4. Contractual Enforceability and Logged-Off Scraping
- *Meta Platforms, Inc. v. Bright Data Ltd.*, No. 3:23-cv-00077-EMC, 2024 WL 245903 (N.D. Cal. Jan. 23, 2024). The court granted summary judgment in favor of the scraper. It held that platform Terms of Service applied to authenticated registered users and did not prohibit logged-off scraping of publicly accessible data.
- *Register.com, Inc. v. Verio, Inc.*, 356 F.3d 393 (2d Cir. 2004). The Second Circuit held that automated software querying with actual knowledge of terms of use can form an enforceable contract if queries are repeatedly submitted after receiving notice of conditions.
- *Southwest Airlines Co. v. Kiwi.com, Inc.*, No. 3:21-cv-00098, 2021 WL 4952481 (N.D. Tex. Oct. 25, 2021). The court enforced terms against a scraper where the defendant had actual knowledge of ToS via cease-and-desist letters and affirmatively agreed to terms during checkout purchases.

### 5. Statutory Search Engine Exceptions in Japan
- Chosakukenhō [Copyright Act], Act No. 48 of May 6, 1970, art. 30-4, as amended by Act No. 30 of 2018 (Japan). The statute permits exploitation of copyrighted works for information analysis and machine learning where the use does not intend to enjoy the creative expression.
- Chosakukenhō [Copyright Act], Act No. 48 of May 6, 1970, art. 47-5, as amended by Act No. 30 of 2018 (Japan). The statute permits minor exploitation of works incidental to computerized information retrieval, search indexing, and directory services, provided that such use does not unreasonably prejudice the economic interests of the copyright owner.
- Minpō [Civil Code], Act No. 89 of 1896, art. 548-2, as amended (Japan). The statute governs the incorporation and enforceability of Standard Form Contracts (Teikei Yakkan).

### 6. Technical Internet Standards
- IETF RFC 9309, *Robots Exclusion Protocol* (Internet Engineering Task Force, Sept. 2022). The standard formalizes the syntax and parsing conventions of `robots.txt`, including the longest prefix matching standard, 24-hour cache lifecycle, and confirming that robots directives represent website operator technical preferences rather than contractual licenses.

### 7. Online Intermediary Regimes and Privacy Frameworks
- 17 U.S.C. Section 512(d). Digital Millennium Copyright Act statutory safe harbor limitation on liability for information location tools, search directories, and hypertext link providers.
- Electronic Commerce Act of 2000, Rep. Act No. 8792, Section 30 (Phil.). The statute supplies limitation of civil and criminal liability for online service providers and search directories.
- Data Privacy Act of 2012, Rep. Act No. 10173, Section 12(f) and Section 16 (Phil.). The statute recognizes legitimate interest processing of publicly available data and establishes statutory data-subject rights.
