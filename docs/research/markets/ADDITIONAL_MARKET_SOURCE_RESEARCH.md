# Additional VRChat market-source leads (2026-09-28)

This is a discovery and design checkpoint, not approval to fetch, retain, or publish data from these hosts. The seven selected node drivers are the **current** capabilities, not a claim that the ecosystem contains only seven sources. No live crawler seed or new driver is enabled by this note. Third-party indexes may create leads, but do not establish a product's authoritative facts. Keep the owner's earlier decision to remove VRCArena from crawler scope; similarity to it does not reinstate it.

**Owner decision (2026-09-28):** public in-game avatars are discovery leads, not accepted catalog items. Their credited authors are strong leads to creator-controlled domains and canonical storefront fronts. Follow explicit, policy-permitted links toward the publisher rather than recursively treating third-party indexes as the catalog's expanding data source. Author credit still requires independent verification before asserting ownership or merging products.

**Owner scope addition (2026-09-28):** include Shopify-based storefronts, including merchant custom domains and potential front catalogs on custom domains, in storefront discovery research. This is a source-class decision, not a blanket approval of every Shopify origin or a claim that every catalog front is publisher-controlled. The distinction between a merchant's own front and a third-party directory remains mandatory.

The intended implementation is one coordinator-leased, bounded discovery step that reports typed product URL leads, followed by separately approved metadata jobs. A discovered sitemap or product URL is not a fetch grant. The existing seven-capability enum has **not** been expanded; Shopify is not yet selected by default, and no Shopify adapter or live-origin profile has been run. A headless site with an explicit public interface can be evaluated as its own source; do not probe for private tokens or assume the Shopify Admin/Storefront APIs are available to this project.

## Architecture implication

Maintain separate `discovery host`, `in-game avatar`, `creator/profile`, `publisher-controlled site`, `storefront front`, and `canonical catalog item` identities. A Nexyy link to Payhip can become a provenance-bearing lead; an avtr.zip listing may likewise lead through an explicit creator/profile link to a self-hosted domain or other storefront. Preserve every hop and its evidence; do not silently treat an inferred creator-name/domain match as a link. Only approved fetching of the destination can create first-party observation evidence. A creator selling the same item on Payhip and Gumroad may have multiple fronts, but must not be merged on title or image alone. A public VRChat avatar ID may relate to a sold source-file product, but is not automatically that product. [VRChat says](https://hello.vrchat.com/avatar-marketplace) Avatar Marketplace sellers may also sell avatars on other marketplaces, which supports researching such relations but does not prove any particular match. The coordinator's hybrid rule system currently auto-queues **VPM listing** leads only; adding Payhip, self-hosted sites, or another kind requires an explicit typed lead-to-job mapping, source policy, and protocol/fixture tests, not a permissive URL rule.

**Protocol checkpoint:** Nodes can now report `creator_profile`, `publisher_site`, and `storefront_product` HTTPS leads through the versioned result schema. An optional `discoveredFromItemKey` anchors a link to one item on a multi-item source page; the coordinator persists it as `discovered_from_item_key`, preserving separate lead identities when two avatars point to the same author site. Older unanchored lead keys remain stable. The coordinator leaves all three kinds pending and cannot fetch or auto-queue them. This preserves candidate host classes and an item anchor, **not** proof that a credited author owns the destination or that the destination sells the specific avatar. Those relation types and approved traversal remain open G3/G4 work.
